Volunteer capture system

Mesa Grove

Great moments happen at church every week, and most of them never get captured. This system gives a team of photo and video volunteers a schedule, clear shot lists, simple camera settings, and one place to put every file, so the comms team always has fresh content to work with.

Ground rules for every volunteer

  • Never photograph a child without a photo consent wristband.
  • Stay out of the aisle and the front during prayer and communion.
  • Candid beats posed. Shoot people doing things, not looking at you.
  • Get faces. A crowd from behind doesn't tell a story.
  • Shoot both horizontal and vertical for every key moment.
  • Upload everything within 24 hours. Don't cull, we'll do that.

Camera cheat sheet

A one-page card for volunteers with church cameras, so the photos look consistent no matter who's shooting. Phone shooters get a simpler version.

Sanctuaryf/2.8 · 1/250

Auto ISO up to 6400. No flash, ever.

Lobbyf/2.8 · 1/200

White balance on auto. Watch for mixed light from windows.

Outdoorsf/4 · 1/500

Shoot with the sun behind people, not in their faces.

Video4K · 30 fps

Hold each shot 10 seconds. Shutter at 1/60.

The asset library

One Dropbox structure for everything, organized by year and then by event. Volunteers only see the upload folders. The comms team handles the edited files and the best-of library that ministries pull from for campus displays.

File naming

Every file is renamed in Lightroom on import, so anyone can find it by date, event and photographer.

2026-11-07_FallFest_AveryL_0142.jpg

Tags we add

Ministry (Kids, Students, Worship), season, and a star rating. Four and five stars go to Best-Of, where they're ready for campus screens, print and social.

Picking images for displays

Real faces, real moments, good light, and a mix of ages and ministries. No one blinking, no one mid-bite, and no kids without consent.

Hand-off workflow

Every batch of photos and video moves across a Monday.com board, so nothing sits forgotten on a memory card.
